MYANMAR ECONOMIC INDICATORS AND INFLATION ANALYSIS 2024

1. Inflation Overview
Myanmar's inflation rate has been volatile due to multiple factors:
- Year-on-Year (YoY) inflation 2024: Estimated 15-20%
- Month-on-Month (MoM) inflation: Averaging 1.2-1.8%
- Core inflation (excluding food and energy): 12-15%
- Food inflation: 18-25% (highest component)

2. Consumer Price Index (CPI) Components
Major CPI basket weights:
- Food and beverages: 45% (largest component)
- Housing and utilities: 15%
- Transport: 12%
- Clothing and footwear: 8%
- Healthcare: 7%
- Education: 5%
- Other goods and services: 8%

3. Key Inflation Drivers
Primary factors contributing to high inflation:
- Currency depreciation (MMK lost 30-40% value against USD in 2023-2024)
- Supply chain disruptions (import restrictions, border closures)
- Fuel price increases (diesel, petrol prices up 50-70% since 2022)
- Agricultural production challenges (weather, input costs)
- Reduced foreign exchange reserves limiting imports

4. Monetary Policy Response
CBM measures to control inflation:
- Policy interest rate: 8.5% (raised from 7% in 2023)
- Reserve requirement ratio: 5% for commercial banks
- Credit growth targets: Limited to 15% annually
- Foreign exchange market intervention (limited due to reserve constraints)

5. GDP Growth Estimates
Economic growth projections:
- 2024 GDP growth: -2% to +1% (contraction or minimal growth)
- Agriculture sector: -5% (weather and input cost issues)
- Manufacturing: -3% (power shortages, import restrictions)
- Services: +2% (telecom, digital services resilient)
- Construction: -8% (reduced investment)

6. Employment and Wages
Labor market conditions:
- Unemployment rate: Estimated 8-12% (unofficial)
- Underemployment: Significant in urban areas
- Minimum wage: MMK 4,800 per day (unchanged since 2018)
- Average monthly salary: MMK 200,000-400,000 (varies by sector)
- Public sector: MMK 150,000-300,000
- Private sector: MMK 300,000-800,000

7. Poverty and Living Standards
- Poverty rate: Estimated 40-50% of population (up from 25% pre-2021)
- Household expenditure: 60-70% on food (high food dependency)
- Rural vs Urban: Rural areas more affected by inflation
- Remittances: Critical income source for 30-40% of households

8. Sectoral Performance
Key economic sectors:
- Agriculture: 25% of GDP, employs 60% of workforce, facing input cost pressures
- Manufacturing: 20% of GDP, power shortages limiting production
- Services: 40% of GDP, digital services growing, tourism collapsed
- Mining: 5% of GDP, gemstones and jade exports restricted
- Construction: 10% of GDP, significant contraction

9. Trade Balance
External trade situation:
- Exports: USD 8-10 billion annually (garments, agriculture, natural gas)
- Imports: USD 12-15 billion annually (fuel, machinery, consumer goods)
- Trade deficit: USD 4-5 billion (financed by remittances, FDI, aid)
- Main export partners: China, Thailand, Japan, EU
- Main import partners: China, Singapore, Thailand, India

10. Fiscal Policy
Government budget constraints:
- Revenue: Limited due to reduced economic activity
- Expenditure: Defense and security spending prioritized
- Budget deficit: Estimated 5-7% of GDP
- Public debt: Growing, limited external financing options

11. Foreign Reserves
- Estimated reserves: USD 1.0-1.5 billion (down from USD 7 billion in 2020)
- Reserve adequacy: Below 3 months of imports (critical level)
- Reserve management: Prioritizing essential imports (fuel, medicine)

12. Remittances
- Annual remittances: USD 2.5-3.0 billion (critical for balance of payments)
- Main sources: Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Middle East
- Remittance channels: Formal (banks, MTOs) and informal (hundi system)
- Impact: Supports household consumption, helps stabilize MMK
